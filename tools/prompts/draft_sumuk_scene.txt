Wide landscape illustration of one scene from a Korean classical tale of the late Goryeo dynasty (Yi Saeng Gyujang-jeon, 'Yi Saeng peeks over the wall'), for a story learning game for high school students.

Scene: a spring afternoon in Songdo (Gaegyeong), the late-14th-century Goryeo capital. A long low garden wall of earth and stone with a small tiled coping runs diagonally across the picture; flowering branches (peach, apricot and plum blossoms) lean over the top of the wall. Inside the wall, on the right side of the picture, is a quiet walled garden of a noble family: flower beds, a small elegant wooden pavilion (a little two-pillar-wide open pavilion with a gently curved tiled roof) and a garden swing hanging from a tree. Near the pavilion stands Ms. Choi, a modest young Goryeo noblewoman of about sixteen, in late-Goryeo dress, not Joseon hanbok: a long hip-length upper jacket with wide sleeves tied at the waist with a long sash ribbon, a long full skirt in soft muted colors, her hair in a low simple chignon at the nape with a thin ribbon. She is gently reading or holding a small book near the flowers, and has just noticed someone, glancing up shyly.
Outside the wall, on the left side of the picture, under a large weeping willow with fresh green spring leaves, stands Yi Saeng, a young Goryeo scholar of about eighteen, in a plain wide-sleeved light-colored scholar robe and a soft black cloth scholar cap (Goryeo style, not the Joseon horsehair gat), a few books tied in a cloth bundle at his side, standing on tiptoe and looking over the wall with curious, surprised eyes.
Only an exchange of glances across the wall; the two are far apart and do not touch. Warm, gentle, wholesome spring mood with drifting petals and soft sunlight. No fighting, no weapons, no fire, no smoke.
Both figures clearly readable; the wall between them is the visual center of the composition.

Art style: a genuine traditional Korean ink-wash painting with light color (sumuk damchae) on hanji paper, in the manner of Joseon literati painters such as Jeong Seon and Kim Hong-do: not a detailed illustration. Loose, confident black ink brushstrokes of varied wetness; the wall, pavilion and willow suggested by a few expressive strokes; the figures drawn with a few simple brush lines, recognizable by pose. Only very light transparent washes of pale peach pink on the blossoms, pale green on the willow, a little ochre and indigo. Generous empty paper (yeobaek) and soft bleeding ink mist. Visible hanji paper fibers. Poetic, airy, quiet. Absolutely no text, no letters, no written characters, no calligraphy, no poem inscription, no seals, no stamps, no signatures, no captions, no watermarks, no frames or borders, no UI, no grid lines.
